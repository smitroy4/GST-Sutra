# GST Registration Rules for Small Shops

## Turnover threshold for goods and services suppliers
Under Section 22(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, a supplier of services (or both goods and services) must register if aggregate turnover in a financial year exceeds Rs. 20 lakh [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 22(1), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section22_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. Under the Third Proviso to Section 22(1) (inserted by Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019, w.e.f. 01-01-2020), the Government may raise this limit up to Rs. 40 lakh for suppliers selling only goods, and the Rs. 40 lakh limit took effect from April 2019 [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 22(1) Third Proviso, https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section22_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20] [Source: PIB NoteId 159075, Nine Years of GST, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=159075&ModuleId=3&reg=20&lang=1, accessed 2026-11-20]. Earning exempt bank interest or discounts does not disqualify a shop from being treated as an exclusive supplier of goods [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Explanation to Third Proviso to Section 22(1), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section22_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20].

## Special Category States turnover thresholds
Under the First Proviso to Section 22(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, the registration threshold is Rs. 10 lakh for Special Category States [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 22(1) First Proviso, https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section22_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. Of the 11 States listed in Article 279A(4)(g) of the Constitution, Explanation (iii) to Section 22 excludes 7 States (Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Meghalaya, Sikkim, and Uttarakhand), giving them a Rs. 20 lakh statutory baseline [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 22 Explanation (iii), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section22_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20] [Source: Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016, Article 279A(4)(g), https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/constitution-amendment-act.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. The remaining 4 Special Category States with a Rs. 10 lakh threshold are Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Tripura [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 22(1) First Proviso & Explanation (iii), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section22_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20].

## Who does not need to register
Under Section 23(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, you do not need GST registration if you exclusively supply goods or services that are not liable to GST or are wholly exempt from GST [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 23(1)(a), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section23_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. An agriculturist is also exempt from registration to the extent of supplying produce out of cultivation of land [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 23(1)(b), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section23_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20].

## Who must register regardless of turnover
Under Section 24 of the CGST Act, 2017, certain businesses must register even if their turnover is below the threshold: persons making any inter-State taxable supply (clause (i)), casual taxable persons such as temporary exhibition sellers (clause (ii)), persons liable to pay tax under Reverse Charge (clause (iii)), and agents supplying taxable goods or services for others (clause (vii)) [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 24(i), (ii), (iii), (vii), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section24_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. Persons supplying goods or services through an e-commerce operator required to collect TCS under Section 52 also fall under Section 24(ix), along with e-commerce operators themselves under Section 24(x) [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 24(ix)–(x), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section24_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. However, from October 2023, small sellers making only intra-State supplies of goods through e-commerce platforms within the turnover threshold are exempt from mandatory registration [Source: PIB NoteId 159075, Nine Years of GST, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=159075&ModuleId=3&reg=20&lang=1, accessed 2026-11-20].

## Basic documents required for a small shop
To apply in FORM GST REG-01, a proprietor needs a PAN (mandatory under Section 25(6)), a valid Indian mobile number, an email address, and an Aadhaar number for authentication under Section 25(6B) [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 25(6) & 25(6B), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section25_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20] [Source: GST Portal Registration Manual — Normal Taxpayer, Part A & Part B, https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/registration/Apply_for_Registration_Normal_Taxpayer.htm, accessed 2026-11-20]. You must upload a photograph of the proprietor (max 100 KB) and proof of your shop address, such as an electricity bill, property tax receipt, municipal khata copy, rent/lease agreement, or consent letter [Source: GST Portal Registration Document Checklist, Proprietorship & Principal Place of Business, https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/cbt/registration/gstregistration/course/story_content/external_files/GST_Registration_Document_Checklist.pdf, accessed 2026-11-20]. Bank account details are not required at application time, but under Rule 10A must be uploaded within 30 days of registration grant or before filing GSTR-1/IFF, whichever is earlier [Source: CGST Rules, 2017, Rule 10A, https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter3/rule10a_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20].

## Time limit for applying and approval speed
Under Section 25(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, you must apply for GST registration within 30 days from the date you become liable [Source: CGST Act, 2017, Section 25(1), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section25_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. A casual taxable person or non-resident taxable person must apply at least 5 days before starting business [Source: CGST Act, 2017, First Proviso to Section 25(1), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section25_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. Under Rule 9(1), normal applications are approved within 7 working days, or up to 30 days if Aadhaar authentication is not done or physical verification is required [Source: CGST Rules, 2017, Rule 9(1), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter3/rule9_v1.00.html, accessed 2026-11-20]. Effective 1 November 2025 under Rule 14A (Notification No. 18/2025-CT dated 31-10-2025), small taxpayers whose monthly output tax on B2B supplies does not exceed Rs. 2.5 lakh can opt for automatic electronic approval within 3 working days using Aadhaar authentication [Source: CGST Rules, 2017, Rule 14A(1) & (4), https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter3/rule_14A.html, accessed 2026-11-20].
